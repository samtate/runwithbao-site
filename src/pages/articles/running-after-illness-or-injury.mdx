Every runner gets ill or hurt sometimes. The worry afterwards is usually the same: how much have I lost, and how soon can I get back to it? The answers are "less than you think" and "sooner than you think, if you build back gently".

## How much have you lost?

A few days off costs almost nothing. After a week you might notice your easy pace feels a little harder. After two weeks or more you'll feel it, but fitness comes back faster than it took to build in the first place, and your legs remember more than your lungs.

<Pull>The mistake isn't taking time off. It's coming back as if you never did.</Pull>

The real risk is trying to make up for it: jumping straight back into the week you'd have been doing, or adding extra runs to catch up. That's how a week off for a cold turns into a month off for a sore shin.

## After a cold or a bug

If it was only a head cold, you may not have needed to stop at all: the [neck rule](/articles/missed-a-run) says a short, easy run is usually fine with symptoms above the neck. If it went to your chest, gave you a temperature or upset your stomach, rest until you feel properly well, then:

- **Start short and easy.** Two or three runs at an easy, chatty pace before anything hard.
- **Expect it to feel harder.** Your heart rate may sit higher for a few days. That's normal.
- **Drink more than usual** after a stomach bug, before and after you run.
- **Give it longer after a fever, flu, Covid or a chest infection.** Ease in over a couple of weeks rather than days.

If you get chest pain, a racing or irregular heartbeat, or you're far more breathless than you should be, stop and speak to your GP before you run again.

## After an injury

Injuries are less predictable than colds, so let pain guide you:

- **Walk first.** If walking briskly hurts, running will too.
- **Run easy, and short.** A run/walk mix is a good first step after a week or more off.
- **Stop if it hurts.** Pain that gets worse as you run, or makes you change how you run, means stop and walk home.
- **Check the next morning.** If it's sorer the next day, it needed more rest.
- **See someone if it lingers.** A physio or your GP can tell you when it's ready, and what to strengthen so it doesn't come back.

## How much to do in the first weeks back

A good rule after a week or more off: start at about three quarters of a normal week, with no hard sessions, and build back over two or three weeks. The longer you were away, the more gently you go.

## Be patient for a fortnight

Two or three easy weeks feel slow when you're keen to get going. They're also what gets you back to where you were without another week off, and that's the quickest way there.
