From November to March, plenty of runs happen indoors: it's dark by five, the pavements are icy, or the gym is the only hour you've got. A treadmill is a perfectly good way to train. It just behaves a little differently from the road, and it helps to know how.

## Why it feels different

- **It's a bit easier at the same speed.** There's no air to push through and no camber, kerbs or hills. A well-known [1996 study](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640419608727717) (Jones and Doust) found that a **1% incline** made treadmill running about as hard as running outside, at speeds from roughly 10.5 to 18 km/h. Slower than that, there's less wind to make up for, so the difference is smaller.
- **The numbers can be off.** Treadmills are rarely calibrated, so the speed and distance on the console may not match the road, especially on older machines. Your watch's indoor distance is a guess from your wrist, and it can be off too.
- **It's warmer.** No breeze means you heat up faster, and your heart rate climbs with it. A fan and a drink close by help more than you'd think.
- **It's the same view.** Half an hour can feel like an hour. Sessions with changes of pace pass much faster than a steady run.

## How to pace it

**Easy runs:** go by feel. You should be able to talk in full sentences. If you use heart rate, it can sit a few beats higher than outside in a warm room, and don't chase the speed you'd run on the road.

**Intervals and tempo:** the treadmill is good at these, because it holds the speed for you. Set the speed for each repeat, and drop it to a jog or walk in between.

**Long runs:** the hardest to do indoors. If you can, take them outside at the weekend in daylight. If you can't, break them into chunks: a few minutes a little quicker, then back to easy, keeps your head in it.

### Does it count?

Yes. An indoor run counts towards your week like any other. If you didn't add a distance, Bao works out what the planned session would have covered in that time at your easy pace, so your next week still builds on what you did.

What an indoor run doesn't do is change your paces much. A belt that's a little off, or a watch guessing from your wrist, shouldn't decide how fast your next tempo run is. So indoor runs only nudge your paces a little, and they're never read as a race result.

## Couch to 5K on a treadmill

Couch to 5K works well indoors: the treadmill keeps you at a steady jog, and you can drop to a walk at the press of a button. Change the speed when you're told to run or walk, rather than jumping on and off the belt.

> **In Bao:** track it with Bao on Android, indoors or out, and the run and walk steps are counted from the timer, so your run counts straight away. If it came from another app or a watch that only saves a summary, Bao can't tell your running from your walking, so it asks: **did you do the whole session?** Your answer is what counts it, and you can change it later.

## Making it bearable

- **Pick the right sessions for indoors.** Intervals, hills on the incline and progression runs suit the treadmill. Save the long, steady ones for outside when you can.
- **Cover the console.** Watching the seconds tick past makes it feel longer. A towel over the screen and something to listen to helps.
- **Get outside sometimes.** Races and parkrun are on roads, paths and grass. Even one outdoor run a week keeps your legs used to the real thing.
